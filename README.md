# Temperature-Shocks-Inflation-Dynamics-and-Monetary-Policy-Transmission-in-India
Objective: To replicate and adapt the analytical framework from Yalçınkaya et al. (2026) for India. The goal is to quantify how temperature shocks impact sectoral and headline inflation in India, and to evaluate the implications for the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) monetary policy framework, particularly in the absence of a formal climate risk integration into its forecasting and supervisory models.

Core Research Questions:

Inflation Impact: Using Indian data (e.g., from CMIE, MOSPI, RBI), what is the empirical relationship between temperature anomalies/shocks and realized inflation across different sub-components (food, fuel, core)? Does the 14-month peak impact found in the US study hold for India, or is the transmission faster/slower given India's economic structure?

Expectations Channel: How do temperature shocks affect inflation expectations across various horizons (3-month, 1-year, 5-year) in India, as measured by surveys like the RBI's Inflation Expectations Survey of Households (IESH)? Does this destabilization effect vary between rural and urban households?

Monetary Policy Blindness: Given the RBI's current monetary policy framework (flexible inflation targeting), how would its interest rate decisions differ if it had ex-ante visibility into climate-driven inflation versus reacting ex-post? Model a counterfactual scenario using historical data.

Supervisory Gap: Where is India's biggest analytical gap regarding climate-related financial risks? Is it in the RBI's credit pricing models for banks (e.g., exposure to climate-vulnerable sectors), its monetary forecasting models (like the Quarterly Projection Model), or its supervisory design for financial institutions?

Methodological Approach:

Use time-series econometrics (e.g., VAR models, local projections) similar to the source paper, applied to Indian data from 1990-2024.

Conduct sectoral analysis, with a deep dive on agriculture, energy, and logistics.

Interview experts from Indian financial institutions, policy think tanks, and academia to qualitatively assess the "analytical gap" (Question 4).

Expected Outputs:

A quantitative report quantifying the inflation sensitivity of the Indian economy to temperature shocks.

A policy brief for the RBI, suggesting how to integrate climate scenario analysis into its supervisory framework and forecasting tools.

A framework for Indian banks to assess climate transition and physical risks in their credit portfolios.
